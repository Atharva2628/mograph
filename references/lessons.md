# Lessons

The kit and the rules come from five rounds of feedback on one film: a 2–3 minute investor film for US 12,173,671 B2, a single-stage-to-orbit rocket engine whose aerospike is made of gas. Each round, what the inventor said, what was wrong, and what changed.

## Round 1 · a dark HUD film, 2:00

What was made: a dark tactical HUD over flat 2D line art, the inventor's own isometric patent figure embedded, a beat-matched music edit of 0:00–2:00.

> "the /anatomy aesthetic i like a lot more … do light theme … some things look very 3d vs some don't … the story and explanations are TOO fast … the boeing story completely went over my head … make each explosion full screen and visually different."

Changes: one isometric renderer for everything; the anatomy paper look for engineering; full-screen failure shots, each a different vehicle and world; the "why" explained step by step; up to 3 minutes.

## Round 2 · light anatomy, re-edited music, 2:58

What was made: everything isometric and light, spiky comic starburst explosions, the song cut into sections and re-ordered (cuts beat-matched to ±16 ms), synthesized sound effects.

> "the audio has a LOT of discontinuations, very bad sounding … keep the previous audio, just adjust speed and start/end times … this animation does not look as detailed … the fluffy cloud shaped explosions would look good … more detail and 3d-ness … more background detail … make it more military tactical looking (Anduril, Caspian Report) … like the missile is weaving through detailed mountain topology … doesn't have to be fully white … commercial rocket → commercial, missile → black ops … some of the models are kinda glitchy … make the company VASE Propulsion and a logo … first person."

Changes: never chop music, play it continuously; fluffy cel explosions; terrain blocks with contours; per-scene worlds; rigid-part vehicles with depth sorting (the glitches were hidden parts showing through); a brand with a meaningful mark; the story in the first person; a terrain-weaving missile scene.

## Round 3 · tactical worlds, continuous music, 2:59

> "this is amazing! during the mountain missile scene, the missile is glitching (head is vibrating) … no need to follow the 3min limit … things are going a lil too fast at the start, kinda hard to read … keyword highlighting for high skimability."

Changes: analytic heading and low-passed altitude (checked numerically); the song before the pre-drop silence stretched to 0.78× with the pitch kept, the switch inside the silence; shorter sentences; captions at the setup; `*keyword*` highlighting.

## Round 4 · 3:16

> "the 'reasons for failure' titles are not in the visual space, cause you're paying attention to the rocket … make the 'booster hung' square more prominent … less time in the rocket equation, use it for the slowed sections … the slowed music is a lil low energy … the reusable landing is upside down … the building windows are messed up … make the vehicle a tad longer and more mature, add a US flag … 'throws itself away' — people might think I don't know about SpaceX reuse … my first idea was revolving an aerospike 360° into a bell, not an aerospike inside a bell … highlight Boeing."

Changes: centered lower-third failure banners and big callouts at the failing part; time moved from the equation to reading-heavy scenes; stretch 0.86× (energy back); VTVL landings upright; face mapping fixed; a longer ogive vehicle with seams, folded legs and a flag decal; the hook rewritten to be true ("Every orbital rocket today breaks itself apart"); the revolve animation; Boeing in a blue name treatment (not their logo).

## Round 5 · 3:09

The cut with every change above was accepted, and the workflow became this skill.

## The short version

1. Find the song's structure; hang the story on it; never chop it; stretch inside silences, ≥ 0.85×.
2. One iso renderer; dense true detail; backgrounds are places; worlds per scene.
3. Put the scene's sentence where the eye is; budget reading time; highlight keywords.
4. Shocking and true; label the illustrative; don't borrow logos.
5. Check stills every act and motion numerically; never overwrite a delivery.
